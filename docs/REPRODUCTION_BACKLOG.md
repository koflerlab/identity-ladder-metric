# Reproduction backlog: literature for the identity-ladder metric

Purpose: independent literature review (no paper draft read) and a list of experiments worth reproducing or borrowing for the claim that pointwise metrics and pointwise-label downstream checks reward regression to the mean in BraTS inpainting. Date: 2026-11-01. Already verified elsewhere and not repeated here: Kofler 2023 (arXiv:2305.08992), Zhang, Weng, Chen 2025 (arXiv:2511.20202), Henschel 2020 FastSurfer, Wang 2004 SSIM, Maier-Hein 2024 Metrics Reloaded, Zhang and Skolnick 2004 TM-score.

Verification: every entry was resolved by DOI or arXiv ID against Crossref (title, authors, year, volume, pages, DOI) and cross-checked with a second source (OpenAlex, PubMed, arXiv, Consensus, or the publisher/proceedings page). The "What it shows" sentence is taken only from the retrieved abstract. Where an abstract was read only in part it is phrased narrowly. Journal abbreviations follow NLM style but were not checked against the NLM catalogue. LNCS volume numbers are omitted because no database confirmed them. Code links marked "repo exists" were confirmed through the GitHub API (existence, licence); whether a repo is the official one is stated only where the abstract or paper says so.

Priority key: high = directly tests or operationalises the claim and has code; medium = supplies a calibration number or a baseline; low = background or theory.

## Papers that challenge our claim

The claim is not contradicted outright by any retrieved abstract. These items limit or complicate it and should be addressed in the paper:

1. Top pointwise scores may not mean blur. Durrer et al. (T2-08, T2-09) report best SSIM/PSNR/MSE for diffusion models, and the fastWDM3D abstract states that earlier DDPM entries gave qualitatively convincing results; the audit must show empirically (mean-fill and blur arms) that leaderboard order really tracks blur, not assume it.
2. The downstream check may be uninformative rather than biased. SynthSeg (T3-02) and the motion-robustness study (T3-07) indicate that modern segmenters are robust to degraded input; this weakens "downstream Dice is a fair arbiter" but also means the bias may be small, so quantify it before claiming it. No paper was found that directly shows blurrier inputs segmenting closer to an oracle; this part of the claim is currently unsupported by literature and needs our own experiment.
3. Realism measures are not a clean fix. T6-02 (medical-trained extractors not better than ImageNet) and T6-03 (FID bias) show that FID-type measures have their own failure modes; T1-10 warns that realism-driven losses can hallucinate.
4. The ladder ordering is not uniform. T5-05 finds MZ<DZ differences only in some regions; T5-09 reports heritability of 0.2-0.5, so the DZ/sibling rung may sit close to strangers; T5-08 reports test-retest ICC of 0.65-0.85, so the same-person floor itself is noisy; T5-10 shows hemispheres are systematically asymmetric.
